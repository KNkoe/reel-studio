# Preferences and hard rules

Everything here came from the creator reviewing real edits. The skill explains how; this
says what they want. Add to it when they give new feedback, and change a rule only when they
say so.

**Using this yourself?** These are the original author's choices. Replace them with yours.

## Story

- Find the point they care about and prove it with their own screens and footage.
- Open with context. A hook lifted from mid-sentence was rejected ("makes no sense").
- Their footage and interactions get more room than the graphics.
- Keep their questions to viewers, calls to action and course plugs.
- In a revision, never silently drop a section or visual they liked. Never clip the ending.
- No hard length cap: an approved reel ran 2:03. Keep the content, cut repetition.

## Picture

- 1080×1920, 30 fps.
- **Full frame** (2026-11-26): approved for sharp, well-lit upright 4K with the corner room
  fitted to each recording's camera angle. The camera is not fixed, so measure every time.
- **Background:** the corner room seen at an angle: window wall one side, wooden table with a
  vase, books and one lamp the other. Calm. No busy shelves or light strips, no flat wall,
  no computer or screen. Nothing placed in front of the speaker.
- **Well-lit takes:** keep their light. Smooth skin, a light colour touch, no relighting.
- **Darker or flat footage** (split screen): studio finish with the copper-gold grade. A
  reference video means copy its techniques, not the presenter or their set.
- Skin clean but not waxy; eyes, brows, lips, beard and clothing stay sharp.
- Never mirror the saved or finished video: writing on clothes must read correctly. Live
  previews move like a mirror.
- The screen recording is never graded.

## Views and looks

Set on 2026-11-27.

- **Three views.** Split (visuals above, speaker below) most of the video. Full face now and
  then, when not explaining something with visuals. Visuals only, with no speaker, only when
  a point is really being stressed, and not often.
- **Interfaces keep the 3D feel.**
- **Visuals made for a video** (explainers and similar screens) follow a style the creator
  keeps private, outside this repository. Do not add it, or a description of it, here.
- **Keep the 3D room** behind the speaker, fitted to the camera's angle.
- Show their event photos where they fit.
- **Check every object properly**, in stills and in motion.

## Split-screen reels

- Head cut out above a rounded face card. The card is edge to edge with a 200 px top radius
  so the curve survives TikTok's side crop. **No space at the sides.** Important text stays
  within x 100–980.
- **Big text only.** No small labels, badge pills, kickers, headers or tags. Headlines
  leave within 4 seconds.
- Lots of motion: camera moves on the 3D screen and face punch-ins.
- Fonts, accent colour and logo come from the product shown, checked in its source.
- Captions: 2–3 word chunks in a white box in the interface's font, without punctuation.
- "Danko!" is their word for thank you; keep their wording.

## Recording

- Use Clapless. Wipe the lens first: it made a clear difference.
- Light on the face; check the preview for stray shadows before recording.
- Upright, waist up. Head at least about 30% of the picture width.
- Check the microphone: the Mac's built-in mic came out far too quiet. Their usual mic is a
  wireless receiver on the Mac.
- Never start a recording without being asked.

## Privacy and cost

- Blur private on-screen text (documents, e-mails, client names, project lists) and ask
  before publishing anything that exposes a vulnerable site.
- Footage stays on the Mac. No paid or upload-based tool without saying the cost and what is
  sent, and getting a yes.
- Transcribe on-device with `scripts/transcribe.swift`; do not download speech models.

## Working with them

- Plain language. Say what was checked and what was not (sampled frames are not "watched").
- New versions are new files. Delete only when asked, after confirming the kept copy.
- Verify pixels and decibels on the rendered file, not on intentions. Contact sheets miss
  short faults: scan frame to frame.
- Look at the footage and ask about the set-up before blaming the camera.
